What I work with.

The toolkit, and what each tool is actually for. Nothing here is listed because it looks good on a CV; each one has done real work.

Product work

Jira
Every story, sub-task and bug. The backlog is the product's memory, so this is where the memory lives.
Confluence
The write-ups behind the tickets: business rules agreed with other departments, feature sets, decisions and why.
Figma
Design canvases that go to review before anything is built, and the shared design language that keeps later modules consistent.
Mailchimp
Lifecycle email journeys for leads at different stages, moved over from the CRM.
Make.com
Automation glue. The practicum tool that turns AI meeting notes into Trello cards and status emails runs on it, with Gemini, Trello and Gmail connected by API.

AI

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, NotebookLM
Research and analysis: triaging literature, drafting, summarising, stress-testing an argument against counter-arguments. Always followed by manual verification, which is the part that makes the speed worth anything.
Cursor and Antigravity
Reading and working with code so I can scope features from what the code does rather than from assumptions; the Flutter, Dart and Firebase app at CIRQL was the proving ground.
Lovable
Quick product prototypes to put in front of people before anyone writes a real line.
Midjourney and ElevenLabs
Image and voice generation for creative and content work, with the same rule: a person reviews everything before it goes out.

Analysis and documents

Excel
Advanced formulae, PivotTables and data analysis. Ten months of coal pricing, logistics costs and supply-chain volumes for Varaha were worked here, and so were payroll and leave schedules back in Cebu.
PowerPoint and Word
Investor-facing decks, briefing notes and summary reports that distil complex commercial information into something decision-ready.
Google Workspace, Teams, OneNote, SharePoint
The collaboration layer: documents, meetings and the shared folders that hold everything else.
Canva
Fast public-facing campaign material, such as posters and adverts converted from policy documents for Citizens UK.

Media

Premiere Pro and After Effects
Filming and editing. The student testimony for the V&A Living Wage campaign was cut here.
Lightroom and Photoshop
Editing the wildlife photographs in the gallery.
Illustrator, InDesign and Acrobat Pro
Print-ready campaign and press material.

Camera

Nikon Z50
With the NIKKOR Z DX 16–50mm f/3.5–6.3 VR kit lens. Small enough to carry everywhere, which is how most of the gallery happened.

This page follows the uses convention: a plain list of what someone works with. It changes as the work does.
