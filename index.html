About

I'm a Product Owner in London, working at the meeting point of product, strategy and politics. At Car Planet UK I own the backlog, roadmap and release testing for a portfolio of in-house web, mobile and AI products used daily across the business, prioritising requests from sales, operations, finance and HR into one roadmap for a single development team. Before that I was a Product Management and Strategy Associate at CIRQL, turning competitor analysis and user feedback into roadmap decisions and driving growth at the coalface.

That product instinct grew out of a political education. I am a Politics graduate of Queen Mary University of London, with Upper Second Class Honours and a dissertation on how disinformation, voter predispositions and contested historical memory shaped the 2022 Philippine presidential election.

Alongside this I serve as Regional Communications, Media and PR Officer for St John Ambulance, have organised in East London with Citizens UK, and spent four years as a freelance open-source investigator. I research, build, write, film and organise.

How I work

Write it down before building it

Every story I hand over carries a user story, Given/When/Then acceptance criteria, what is out of scope, and the teams it depends on. Big pieces get split into sub-tasks the developers can actually estimate.

In practice

200+ Jira tickets in my first eight weeks at Car Planet UK, with every story written this way and the large pieces broken into sub-tasks the team could estimate.

Verify before describing

Before a ticket says what the product does today, I check the product's own data. Guessing at current behaviour is how bugs get written into requirements.

In practice

At CIRQL I read the app's Flutter, Dart and Firebase code with AI assistance before scoping features, which cut scoping time by about a working day and let me argue feasibility from the code rather than from assumptions.

Design first, then build

A redesign starts as a canvas the team can review, and the decisions get captured as a shared design language, so the next module is consistent with the last without anyone re-deciding.

In practice

At Car Planet UK a module redesign went to review as a design canvas before any build, and delivery was re-sequenced design-first after that review.

AI for speed, people for judgement

I use AI to compress research, drafting and code reading, and keep a human check on everything it touches: manual verification on the dissertation, review for audience fit on campaign material, and legal and data-protection review before any customer data is used.

In practice

Dissertation research moved about 40 per cent faster with manual verification throughout. Citizens UK campaign materials came together about 25 per cent faster, with every piece reviewed for audience fit. A call-deflection pilot for an AI assistant was scoped with legal and data-protection review before a single transcript was used.
